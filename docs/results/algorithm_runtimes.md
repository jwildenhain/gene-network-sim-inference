# How long each algorithm takes

Generated by `scripts/render_runtimes.py` from stored results (`archive_leaderboard.json`, `audit_100_250.json`, `timings_500/audit_modular_500.json`); `tests/test_runtimes_doc.py` fails when this page and the data differ. Rows are ordered from fastest to slowest at 500 genes.

## What the numbers are

- **Time** is the algorithm's own fit time on one network, the figure the studio shows as run time (an ensemble's time includes its members). It excludes simulating the network, scoring and drawing; a run in the studio or the job queue adds those, and simulating a network takes seconds up to a minute or two at 500 genes.
- **Where they come from:** *up to 16 genes* and *500 / 1000 genes (archive)*: the median over the archived runs of that size (471, 13 and 1 runs; the 1000-gene column is a single run). *100 / 250 / 500 genes (audit)*: one fresh process per algorithm on one `replogle_like` modular network (not isolated from other load on the machine, so single entries can be off by a factor of two).
- **Machine:** AMD Threadripper 2990WX (64 threads), BLAS pinned to one thread, process pools up to 16 workers for the algorithms that have one (S2G, G2S, the voting ensembles, GENIE3, GRNBoost-lite, NIR). A laptop with fewer cores will be slower by about the core ratio for those; the others are single-threaded. Concurrent jobs slow each other down; a busy machine can double a time.
- **Growth** is the exponent between the 500- and 1000-gene archive medians (time ~ genes^growth); 3 means doubling the genes multiplies the time by 8. Use it to extrapolate cautiously: the project's scope stops at 500 genes.

## Reading

- **Up to 16 genes everything is instant** (the slowest, GRNBoost-lite, takes under a second), so the whole leaderboard of the studio and the sweeps cost seconds per network.
- **At 500 genes the cost splits in two groups.** `atlasshift`, `mean_difference`, `gene_features`, `pearson`, `by_chance`, `cpa_lite`, `sfinn`, `dmdgrn`, `fast`, `netdeconv`, `deeprig`, `arachne`, `s2g`, `ridge_cv`, `causalgrn`, `cellbox`, `llc_mean`, `g2s`, `notears`, `inspre` finish in under 10 s each. `genie3`, `nir`, `grnboost`, `vote`, `score_vote` take 10 s to a few minutes; all of them together need roughly 2.2 min per network (archive medians), most of it in the voting ensembles, GRNBoost-lite, NIR and GENIE3.
- **The steepest growth is NOTEARS** (a matrix-exponential constraint) and G2S; at 1000 genes NOTEARS needs about 5.9 min and the voting ensembles about 4.7 min.
- **Memory** at 500 genes stays within about 1.5 GB above the process baseline for every algorithm (CellBox is the largest).
- Times of the stored results come from the code of their time; FAST and GRNBoost-lite were recomputed for 1.0.5, CellBox only up to 266 genes (see CHANGELOG), so a few 500- and 1000-gene entries differ from the audit column. Use the order of magnitude.
