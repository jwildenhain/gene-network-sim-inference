# Partial Perturb-seq screens across network sizes and fractions perturbed

Generated by `python scripts/render_partial_grid.py` from `docs/results/partial_grid/` (grid 1: `grid_<n>.json`; grid 2:
`impute_<n>_<pct>pct.md`). Raw logs are next to them. Run with grid code at v1.0.4 (NOTEARS with the new penalty path).

**Setup.** `replogle_like` networks of 100 and 250 genes (500 genes were dropped for now); 6%, 20%, 50% and 75% of the genes
perturbed (knockdown, 100 cells per perturbation, genes chosen at random). Grid 1 uses 5 seeds and runs every registered algorithm
as it is ("native") and, for the Jacobian-type ones, through `PartialAdapter` ("adapted"); the five that need a square screen
(s2g, g2s, vote, score_vote, netdeconv) are only usable adapted. Grid 2 uses 3 seeds and six methods and asks whether the missing
perturbations can be filled in from three *source* contexts (the same network with different basal rates, each a full single-cell
screen with the target's noise) before the algorithm runs; with and without 30% of the target's edges rewired. All numbers are AUPR
against the direct edges of the target; chance is 0.01-0.03.

## Findings

1. **More perturbed genes helps the best methods, not all of them.** The best balanced score (mean of P->P and P->unperturbed AUPR)
   rises from about 0.5 at 6% to 0.52-0.64 at 20-75%, carried by cellbox and the consensus of inspre, mean_difference, bicycle_lite
   and dotears_lite. mean_difference, inspre and pearson stay roughly flat.
2. **No single winner.** cellbox is best at 20% and 50% at 100 genes and at 50% at 250 genes; the consensus is best at 75% at both
   sizes and at 6% at 250 genes; nir leads at 20% at 250 genes. Gaps between neighbours are often inside the seed spread (the sd is
   in the tables), so only the grouping is reliable: cellbox, the consensus, nir, s2g (adapted) and mean_difference form the top tier.
3. **Methods differ by block.** Response-only methods (pearson, causalgrn, genie3, arachne) score well on P->unperturbed edges and
   near chance on P->P; adapted ridge_cv is strongest on P->P. The balanced score hides this, so use the A and B columns.
4. **NOTEARS gets worse as more genes are perturbed** (100 genes: 0.20, 0.19, 0.14, 0.10 balanced from 6% to 75%; 250 genes: 0.13,
   0.12, 0.09, 0.06), and is below most methods at every point. Cause not investigated.
5. **Filling in the missing perturbations from other contexts lets every method score edges whose regulator was never perturbed**
   (hidden blocks C and D: about 0.01-0.05 on the partial data, 0.5-0.6 imputed when the target matches the sources). It does not
   improve the observed blocks much (+0.0 to +0.2), and the gain shrinks as the fraction perturbed grows, because less is left to fill.
6. **The gain depends on how well the sources match the target.** With 30% of the edges rewired the imputed hidden-block score falls
   to 0.28-0.35, about the level of the sources alone, and at 250 genes the imputed observed-block score falls below the partial one
   at 50-75% perturbed (0.31 vs 0.36 at 50%). Averaged over fractions, the all-pairs gain over the partial data is +0.20 to +0.55
   with matching sources and +0.09 to +0.29 with 30% rewiring.
7. **Caution on the 0%-rewired rows.** There the sources have the target's exact network and are three full screens pooled, so the
   "sources" and "imputed" scores exceed the single-screen "oracle". That says transfer works when the contexts share a network, not
   that imputation beats measuring the perturbations. The all-pairs partial score is low mainly because hidden-regulator pairs
   cannot be scored from a partial screen at all (they sit at chance).

## Limits

100 and 250 genes only; 5 seeds (grid 1) and 3 seeds (grid 2), so differences of a few hundredths are noise. At 100 genes with 6
perturbed, block A has only 6 possible regulators (chance level about 0). Random gene choice, 100 cells per perturbation. Grid 2 covers
six methods, not all 24. The source contexts are synthetic (same network, different basal rates), not real atlases.
