GRN-Sim / phpIoRN

Simulate a gene network, perturb it, then try to recover it

A workbench for reverse-engineering gene regulatory networks from perturbation experiments, with ground truth you control. This deck explains the tool, each way it can simulate data, and what is distinct about each of its 24 inference algorithms.

1 · Simulate

Hill-kinetics ODE networks, four topologies, four dynamics engines, three response regimes, single-cell Perturb-seq screens.

2 · Infer

24 registered algorithms from the thesis family (S2G, G2S, NIR, FAST) to causal, tree, neural and transfer methods.

3 · Evaluate

Edge recovery against the truth, held-out perturbation prediction, rewiring sensitivity, and real benchmarks (E. coli SOS, Norman, DREAM4).

The data

What an experiment produces: two square matrices

Each gene is perturbed once. Column j of X is the whole network's steady-state response to perturbing gene j; U records what was done.

Linearising around the reference steady state gives the equation every thesis-family algorithm inverts:

  • A is the Jacobian: its sparsity pattern and signs are the network to recover.
  • Fewer columns than genes is a partial screen (only some genes perturbed).
  • Orientation differs between algorithms; grn_sim.inference.orientation normalises it before scoring.
The simulator core

Each gene is an ODE with Hill-saturated regulation

Production of gene i is a basal rate multiplied by saturating terms from its activators and inhibitors; degradation is first order.

  • v basal rate, b degradation, K half-saturation, m Hill coefficient (cooperativity).
  • Activators raise production up to 2×; inhibitors cut it towards 0.
  • Thesis defaults: v ≈ 1.05, b ≈ 0.75, K 1-2.5, m 1-6.
  • Silent edges: if K is far from the regulator's level the term is flat and the edge cannot be seen in data. Calibrated presets anchor K to the steady state to avoid this.

Drag K and m: a steeper curve (larger m) switches sharply near K; a regulator whose level sits on a flat part transmits almost nothing.

Interdependencies

Perturb one gene, watch the network settle

Dependencies are not drawn from a correlation model. They come from the ODEs: the change in one gene alters the production of its targets, which alter theirs, until a new steady state. Try it on this 6-gene network (a tiny Hill simulator running in your browser).

mode

Bars show Xi = xi/xref − 1. Green edges activate, red edges inhibit. In input mode the perturbed gene's own level stays put (only its targets see the change); in knockdown it drops first. G6 responds although nothing touches it directly: that indirect effect is what the inference algorithms have to see through.

Simulation option

Three ways to perturb a gene

default

input

Adds perturbation_factor to gene j's concentration as seen by its regulators (the thesis "tagged mRNA" device).

Gene j's own dynamics stay untouched, so regressions do not mistake the perturbation for self-regulation. U[j,j] = +p. Used by the Studio and VCC "Activation".

CRISPRi-like

knockdown

Scales gene j's production by 1 − e; default e = 0.8, with optional per-gene scatter (efficiency_spread).

U[j,j] = −e. The target's own drop dominates its profile, so score on off-target genes (offtarget_r).

complete loss

knockout

Production set to zero (efficiency 1).

U[j,j] = −1. In single-cell screens, biallelic loss (80%) versus half dose is modelled per cell.

The Studio uses input; the VCC tab lets you choose input or knockdown; the Perturb-seq pipeline offers all three.
Simulation option · topology

Four network topologies

Mean connectivity k_av sets the edge count (ceil(k_av·n)); act_ratio sets the activating share. The topology sets who connects to whom.

random

Poisson degrees (Erdős-Rényi): one random regulator per gene, then random extra edges. No hubs, no structure.

power_law

Scale-free: links go to nodes in proportion to their degree, so a few hubs dominate. Hubs centred, low-degree genes outside.

hierarchical

A small scale-free block cloned subdiv times, plus cross-block edges. Identical blocks, drawn top-down (regulators above targets).

modular

1+Poisson(k_av−1) regulators per gene, 85% from its own module. Dense modules, few bridges: the Perturb-seq regime.

Generated samples (20 genes, k_av 1.8, 60% activating). Node size = degree; green arrow activates, red bar inhibits; dashed = edge between modules.

Simulation option · kinetics_engine

Four ways to generate steady states

default

hill_nonlinear

Numerical integration of the Hill ODEs until the squared displacement is below 10-4. The reference model the thesis algorithms were built for; saturation makes responses non-linear in the perturbation.

cellbox_neural_ode

The CellBox model class: W = γ·T, tanh saturation instead of Hill terms. Data suits CellBox and linear methods differently than Hill data.

linear_jacobian

Closed form, no integration. Adjacency scaled to spectral radius below 1. Here A X = −U is exact, so it is the easiest setting for regression and inversion methods.

perturb_seq_counts

Hill steady states first, then 400 cells per condition with overdispersion and log-normal library depth; X is rebuilt from the cell averages. Gives .h5ad-style count data.
Simulation option · dynamics_preset, noise

Responsive, flat or oscillating, plus measurement noise

responsive

default

Clear steady-state shifts after each perturbation. What the algorithms are designed for.
flat

nothing to see

Perturbation factor 0.005, noise 0.001: changes below 5%. Shows what inference does with no signal.
oscillating

limit cycle

A negative-feedback ring (Hill m 4.5, v 8) never settles. The run is labelled non-steady and Perturb-seq refuses it.
Measurement noise (noise, default 0.05, applied to each observed steady state):
Biological variability (bio_var) is stored with a Studio run but the Studio does not currently pass it to the simulator. PerturbationSimulator(biological_variability=...) implements it for Python use.

Panel 4

Sources, a rewired target and held-out perturbations

  • Seen / held-out split. 75% of the target's perturbations are seen; the last 25% (at least 2) are held out. Every model is refit without the held-out columns.
  • Linear predictor R = sign·pinv(A_est) is exactly 0 for a held-out gene (its row of A is never identified).
  • Hill refit predictor keeps each algorithm's top-2 regulators per target and refits a Hill model on the seen data.
  • References: mean-profile null, true-structure oracle, target-gene prior (knockdown), cross-context transfer.
  • Also computed: epistasis for every gene pair, virtual single cells with PCA, and prediction decay as rewiring grows 0-50%.

ε > 0.45 synergistic / synthetic lethal, > 0.25 intermediate, else additive.

Single-cell simulation

A pooled CRISPR screen with ground truth

1 ConditionsSteady states for every single (and double) perturbation; truth: total log2 effects, edge elasticities, labels, epistasis.
→
2 CellsGuides with efficiencies, per-cell doses, escapers, knockout alleles; extrinsic noise propagated through the network (LNA or SDE).
→
3 MeasurementDoublets, ambient RNA, capture bias, batches, library size, NB UMI sampling, guide calling.
→
4 RealismStatistics versus genome-scale targets; calibrated presets.

Cell noise model

  • lna linear-noise approximation around each steady state: fast, accurate for well-expressed genes (default).
  • sde chemical-Langevin integration of every cell (Euler-Maruyama): exact, much slower; differs for low-copy genes and steep Hill curves.

Design options

  • mode knockdown / knockout / input, pairs for double perturbations.
  • Partial screens: perturb 30 genes, measure 500, with effective or random selection.
  • Presets: thesis, replogle_like (modular, anchored kinetics, 80% activating).
Output is an AnnData .h5ad with counts, per-cell truth in obs and the ground truth in uns["ground_truth"].
Scoring

How an inferred network is judged

  • Coverage / power: share of true edges found. FDR: share of calls that are wrong. F1 and total errors summarise both.
  • 9-state classification: each (regulator, target) pair is true +/-/0 and predicted +/-/0, so sign errors are visible, not hidden as hits.
  • Orientation. Algorithms emit A[target, regulator] or [regulator, target]; the registry declares which and orient_prediction normalises it before scoring.
  • Total versus direct effects. A perturbation also moves downstream genes; direct-edge truth and total-effect truth are scored separately.

Reading the numbers honestly

  • By-Chance is the null every method must beat.
  • 3-4 seeds gave wide spreads: treat docs/results as orders of magnitude, not rankings.
  • Knockdown data: score off-target genes, the target gene dominates.
  • Real data (SOS, Norman) usually show much smaller gains over chance than the simulator does.
Algorithms

24 registered algorithms in five families

All take fit(X, U) and return A_est (scores) and adjacency_pred (signed -1/0/+1). They differ in the assumption that lets them turn responses into edges.

Regression on perturbation data

8

S2G, G2S, Vote, Score Vote, NIR, FAST, RidgeCV, By-Chance. Solve A X = −U row by row with different sparsity rules.

Response-matrix & transfer

5

inspre, Mean difference, Network Deconvolution, DMD-GRN, AtlasShift. Work on the effect matrix itself: invert, deconvolve or transfer.

Co-expression & statistical

5

Pearson, GENIE3, GRNBoost-lite, ARACNE, Gene features. Rank pairs by association; the perturbation mostly gives direction, if anything.

Causal & DAG

2

CausalGRN, NOTEARS. Impose a causal structure: partial correlation plus interventions, or an acyclicity constraint.

Neural & latent

4

CellBox, DeepRIG, SFINN-style, CPA-Lite. Learn a parametric model or embedding and read influence from it.

Experimental (3)

not in registry

bicycle-lite, DOTEARS-lite, Rank consensus: cell-level, interventional-aware prototypes.

Regression · thesis core · s2g

Simple-to-General: grow the regulator set until the penalty says stop

  1. Start with no regulators; residual E = −yi.
  2. Project E on all (unit-norm) basis columns; add the best match.
  3. Refit by pseudo-inverse; swap out the weakest term if it is not the one just added.
  4. Evaluate the cost; stop when it no longer drops.

What is distinct

  • No fixed number of regulators per gene; the description-length penalty decides.
  • Forward selection with backtracking (Judd & Mees basis pursuit).
  • Best of the thesis family on 100-gene simulated screens (AUPR 0.35).
  • Needs PartialAdapter for partial screens.

Crampin et al. 2004; Wildenhain thesis ch. 3.

Regression · thesis core · g2s

General-to-Specific: start with every gene, prune the least useful

  1. Fit with all n genes as regulators.
  2. For each candidate, refit without it and take the one whose removal raises the squared error least.
  3. Repeat; stop when AIC stops improving.

What is distinct

  • Backward elimination: the mirror of S2G, with a different error profile (its false positives are largely uncorrelated with S2G's).
  • A fast exact engine reproduces the MATLAB result and runs on a process pool.
  • Rank-deficient X makes the default fast path differ from MATLAB; the exact path matches.
Regression · thesis core · nir, fast

Fix K regulators per gene: search them (NIR) or invert and keep the top K (FAST)

NIR (Gardner et al. 2003)

  • Exactly K inputs per gene (K ≈ k_av).
  • Beam search (top-D) over regulator subsets, ranked by SSE.
  • Rejects ill-conditioned subsets (cond > 1000); optional ridge and BIC order choice.
  • For unperturbed genes regresses their own response on the others, so it takes partial screens as they are.

FAST (Farina et al. 2003)

  • Truncated SVD (drop the smallest singular value) gives a full A0 in one shot.
  • Keep the K largest entries per row, then refit those by least squares.
  • Fast and deterministic; no search, but sensitive to the truncation.
Both expect the perturbation sign convention −U at fit time (FIT_INPUT_SIGN); the benchmark code handles this.
Regression · combiners and controls

Voting, evidence voting, ridge and the random null

core

Voting Ensemble

Intersection of S2G and G2S (and NIR on Hill data): few false positives, lower recall than either member.
extended

Score Vote

Each member contributes the t-statistic of the edge coefficient, mapped to a z-score; z-scores are summed with a correction for the members' correlation. Keeps how strong each call is instead of a yes/no.
baseline

RidgeCV

Multi-output L2 regression, α by cross-validation (Kaggle NeurIPS 2023 baseline). In additive fits the perturbed gene's own term must be a regressor or the fit is near random.
null model

By-Chance

Random network with the right connectivity and activator ratio. Ignores the data: the floor every method must clear.
Response-matrix & transfer methods

Work on the effect matrix itself

extended

inspre

Total effects R become direct effects G via a sparse inverse (ADMM, L1). Handles feedback loops if stable; accepts partial screens and per-edge priors.
baseline

Mean difference

The unnormalised effect matrix (a CausalBench winner). Scores total effects, so it is perfect against a total-effect truth by construction.
extended

Network Deconvolution

Removes transitive indirect effects via an eigenvalue map (Feizi 2013).
extended

DMD-GRN (Koopman)

Controlled dynamic-mode decomposition: a truncated-SVD best-fit linear operator, off-diagonal entries discretised.
transfer

AtlasShift

Empirical, not mechanistic: fuses the same perturbation observed in other cell contexts. Port of a VCC 2026 leaderboard entry. As a structure scorer it reads A[j,i] = response of i to perturbing j.

Why these differ

inspre and ND try to separate direct from indirect effects; Mean difference and AtlasShift deliberately do not. Which truth you score against (direct or total) decides who wins.

Co-expression & statistical

Rank pairs by association, add direction from the perturbation

baseline

Pearson |r|

Absolute correlation across conditions. Symmetric and blind to which gene was perturbed: the cheapest control.
extended

GENIE3

Extra-trees per target; edge weight is feature importance; sign from correlation. DREAM4/5 winner. Non-linear, but needs many conditions.
baseline

GRNBoost-lite

Gradient-boosted trees per target, importance-ranked (the GRNBoost2 recipe via scikit-learn, not the Dask code). Few conditions and many candidates limit it.
extended

ARACNE

Gaussian-copula mutual information with Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage; the data-processing inequality removes the weakest edge of every triangle (indirect links); direction from perturbation.
control

Gene features

How much a moves anything times how much j moves under anything, ignoring the pair. Shows how much of a score is just hubness.

Common limit

Association is symmetric and cannot tell direct from indirect. These shine with many conditions; in a one-gene-per-run screen they trail the interventional methods.

Causal & DAG

Impose a causal structure

extended

CausalGRN

First-order partial correlations drop edges explained by a third gene (the skeleton). The perturbed gene gives each edge's direction (a do-calculus style rule).
extended

NOTEARS

Continuous optimisation of a weighted graph with a smooth acyclicity constraint (augmented Lagrangian). Cannot represent feedback loops, which real regulatory networks (and this simulator) contain.

CausalGRN

Assumes

  • Few common causes; first-order partial correlation is enough to explain away an edge.
  • The perturbed gene is a cause of the others, never an effect of them.

Fails when

  • Explanations need two or more conditioning genes.
  • Indirect effects look as strong as direct ones.

NOTEARS

Assumes

  • The true graph is acyclic and the model is a linear SEM.
  • Every column of X is a clean sample of that SEM.

Fails when

  • Feedback exists (Hill networks often have it).
  • Responses are non-linear: the linear fit blurs saturation.
Experimental relatives: DOTEARS-lite (NOTEARS with the intervened gene's own equation dropped in its cells) and bicycle-lite (steady-state Ornstein-Uhlenbeck likelihood).
Neural & latent-space

Learn a model, read influence from it

extended

CellBox (neural ODE)

Fits steady states with L1-penalised weights (PyTorch, SciPy fallback up to 200 genes). Inputs are rescaled by 1/std(X): log fold changes near 0.1 otherwise leave tanh in its linear range and the fit learns nothing.
extended

CPA-Lite

A small autoencoder whose latent space adds perturbation vectors; gene influence from its Jacobian. Simplified, not the published model. Its A is a latent projection, so it has no steady-state response operator.
extended

DeepRIG

Graph autoencoder on a covariance-shrunk co-expression prior graph with a low-rank bottleneck; pair scores from the inner-product decoder.
extended

SFINN-style

Despite the name a deterministic rule: perturbation sensitivity S weighted by a shared-factor association G (correlation after smoothing conditions over nearest neighbours).

Try it

From Python:

net = GeneNetwork.create(n=12, k_av=1.8, net_type=POWER_LAW, seed=1) exp = PerturbationSimulator(perturbation_factor=2.0)\ .run(net, seed=1) res = SimpleToGeneral().fit(exp.X, exp.U) evaluate_inference(exp.true_matrix, res.adjacency_pred)

From the shell: grn-sim web, or grn-sim perturbseq --genes 500 --preset replogle_like --out screen.h5ad.
